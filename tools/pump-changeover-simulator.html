Pump Changeover Simulator

Two identical centrifugal pumps on a common header. Pump A is running; pump B is about to be started for a changeover. This checks whether B's check valve will lift against the header, where both pumps sit during the overlap relative to minimum flow, and how hot a deadheaded pump gets under the protection layers you select. Pump data comes from the Centrifugal Pump Sizing tool (its default pump, or the one you hand over from it). US units only in this version.
Scope: single-stage, radial/mixed-flow centrifugal pumps, the same screen the sizing tool applies (10–10,000 gpm, 15–1,000 ft, Ωs 0.2–1.6). Multistage, axial-flow and positive-displacement duties are refused. Header flow is limited to 30–130% of rated (the computed curve) and wear-ring clearance ratios to 1–4.

How do I determine c/c₀?

What it is. c/c₀ = current diametral wear-ring clearance ÷ the as-new clearance. Karassik (§2.1.3, p.2.131): "replace or repair wearing rings when the nominal clearance has doubled." So 2.0 is the most a pump on a working PM program should reach.

Why it matters here. At low header flow, A runs near the flat top of its curve, so B lifts by only a few percent of head. Wear cuts B's shutoff head by ~3.4% at c/c₀ = 2 (Table 3), which can be enough to keep its check valve shut. The worst pairing is a freshly repaired A against an unrepaired B.

Pump A (running). It can't be measured in service, and it doesn't need to be. Run it at both bounds:

  • 1.0 for B's lift check. A new A holds the header at its highest head, which is the hardest case for B.
  • Worn (e.g. 2.0) for A's own minimum flow. A worn A gets pushed further back on its curve during the overlap.

  • c₀: the vendor's cross-section or data book. Failing that, Karassik Fig. 48 (§2.2.1, p.2.275) gives the new diametral clearance against leakage-joint diameter for non-galling pairs. A 9.000 in ring gives 0.018–0.024 in; add 0.002–0.004 in for galling metals such as chrome steels.
  • Wear since the last repair follows run hours, not calendar time. A true standby wears little; pumps alternated on a schedule wear about equally.

When the rings can be measured. On back-pull-out overhung pumps, most seal and bearing repairs pull the rotor, which exposes the impeller ring and the casing-ring bore. Routine PMs (lube, vibration, alignment) don't open the casing. Recording as-found and as-left ring clearance on those repair work orders is what makes a measured value available later.

Inferring it from a field test is weak. At c/c₀ = 1.78, Table 3 shows shutoff head down only 3% and BEP head down 1.7%. That's within gauge, speed and impeller-erosion error. A test can tell "about new" from "badly worn" (3.5 or more), but not 1.5 from 2.

Basis. The head factors come from Karassik §2.1.3 Table 3, which is one Flowserve pump (ns 2100, 63 ft); other pumps in that table respond differently. The tool stops at 4: twice the replacement point, which already covers the abrasive or overdue case.
